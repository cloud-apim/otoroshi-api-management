# Payments

**The product decides access; Stripe decides the money.** The product takes no payment, computes
no invoice, handles no tax and stores no means of payment. Nothing of it is in the request path:
the gateway sees an apikey, enabled or disabled — if the product is down, the traffic flows, the
one of a customer whose payment just failed included, until the next projection.

## The Stripe account of a workspace

A workspace sells its paid plans through **its own Stripe account**, linked by **Stripe Connect**:
**Workspace settings › Payments › Link a Stripe account** sends one of its administrators to Stripe,
who authorises the installation and comes back. The product keeps the id of the account, its name
and whether it is live — never a key of it; it calls Stripe with the key of the installation, on
behalf of the account. Unlinking is refused while paid subscriptions are open; an account that
takes its authorisation back from Stripe is unlinked.

The installation sets, once:

| Variable | |
|---|---|
| `APIM_PAYMENTS_PROVIDER` | `stripe`, `fake` (the development and the tests) or `off` (the default without a Connect client id) |
| `APIM_STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` | the secret key of the platform's Stripe account |
| `APIM_STRIPE_CLIENT_ID` | the Connect client id (`ca_…`) |
| `APIM_STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` | the secret of the Connect webhook endpoint (`whsec_…`) |
| `APIM_STRIPE_API_VERSION` | the version of the Stripe api the product speaks, `2025-03-31.basil` |
| `APIM_PAYMENTS_RECONCILE_MS` | how often the paid subscriptions are read again from Stripe, every hour |

**The webhook**: one endpoint for every linked account, `<public url>/payments/stripe/webhook`,
registered once in the Stripe dashboard of the platform as a **Connect** endpoint, with the events
`checkout.session.completed`, `customer.subscription.updated`, `customer.subscription.deleted`
and `account.application.deauthorized`.

## Paying a plan

1. On the portal, a consumer subscribes an application to a paid plan: the subscription waits in
   `pending_payment`.
2. **Pay** opens a **Stripe Checkout**, a page of Stripe, on the account of the workspace. The
   price is the plan's — its amount, currency and interval — unless the plan names a price at
   Stripe; the id of the subscription goes in the metadata.
3. Stripe tells the product (`checkout.session.completed`): the subscription becomes active, and
   only then its key is projected on the gateway.

**The team pays.** Its billing account at Stripe — one per team and Stripe account — groups the
invoices of its applications. **Invoices and card**, on the application in the portal, opens the
**customer portal of Stripe**: invoices, means of payment, cancellation. The product has no screen
for them.

## What follows

The subscription follows what Stripe says of its Stripe subscription:

| At Stripe | The subscription | Its key |
|---|---|---|
| `active`, `trialing` | active, paid | enabled |
| `past_due` — a payment failed, Stripe retries | active, past due | enabled, for the **grace period** of the plan at most |
| past due beyond the grace period | **suspended** for want of payment | disabled |
| `unpaid`, `incomplete_expired`, `paused` | suspended for want of payment | disabled |
| paid again | resumed — what a producer suspended stays suspended | enabled |
| `canceled` | closed | deleted |

The other way round, **closing** a paid subscription in the product cancels it at Stripe, and a
**move** between two paid plans changes its price at Stripe, prorated. A move from a free plan to a
paid one goes through a payment: the consumer subscribes to the paid plan.

## Lost webhooks, replayed ones

A webhook is **checked by its signature** (and its age: five minutes, against a replay), **kept
once** by its id — Stripe redelivers what it is not sure was received — and handled by the
projection queue, so that a failure is retried. Every event and what it did is listed in
**Workspace settings › Payments**.

A webhook can also be lost: the product **reconciles** its paid subscriptions with Stripe every
hour (and on demand, **Reconcile now**), the way it reconciles with the gateway.

## In the development

`APIM_PAYMENTS_PROVIDER=fake` — the default of the docker stack — stands for Stripe: linking an
account answers at once, a Checkout is a page of the product that pays or cancels, and the events
are the ones Stripe would send, signed, on the same endpoint. `mise run demo` links it to the
default workspace. The product refuses to start with it when `NODE_ENV=production` — the setting
of its image.
