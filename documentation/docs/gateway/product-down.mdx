# When the product is down

The product describes the offer and projects it; Otoroshi enforces it. The product is never asked,
call by call, whether a consumer may pass. What that means during an incident, and what is left
once the product is uninstalled, is checked on a real stack by `test/product-down.test.js`
(`mise run test:e2e`).

## The product is down

With its servers and its databases stopped, Otoroshi keeps serving everything deployed:

- the keys of the subscriptions, the tokens checked by the derived JWT verifiers and auth modules,
  the certificates trusted through the derived CA certificates;
- the quotas and the rate limits of the plans: the gateway counts them on the keys;
- the data exporter keeps trying to reach the ingester; the traffic does not wait for it.

What stops with the product is the product itself: the console, the developer portal, the CLI, new
subscriptions, the analytics, the drift detection.

## The gateway is unreachable

The product keeps working on its side:

- the developer portal serves its catalogue and its documentation, and the console reads;
- a subscription accepted meanwhile is recorded; its projection waits in the
  [queue](./projection.mdx#asynchronous-and-replayable), retried with a backoff, and reaches the
  gateway once it is back — its credentials appear then;
- the **Gateway** page shows the gateway unreachable and the tasks that wait.

## Taking the configuration over by hand

With the product down, or gone, an Otoroshi administrator manages the configuration with Otoroshi
alone, through its admin API or its backoffice:

- **revoke a consumer**: disable its key;
- **subscribe a consumer**: an `ApiSubscription` on an apikey or oauth2-local plan, and Otoroshi
  mints its key from the plan;
- **change the offer**: edit a plan of the `Api`, and Otoroshi carries its quotas to the keys of
  its subscriptions.

While an `Api` carries the marker of the product (`created_by: external=otoroshi-api-management`),
Otoroshi takes it for an api managed as code: each write that changes what is live is a deploy,
recorded in the deployment history of the API. Remove `created_by` from its metadata to manage it
through Otoroshi's own draft and deploy.

Back, the product reports what was changed by hand as [drift](./drift-and-adopt.mdx), and puts its
description back on demand, by redeploying the snapshot. What was created by hand — a subscription,
its key — is not the product's: it is left alone.

## Uninstalling

Uninstalling the product is stopping it and dropping its two databases. The traffic needs nothing
else. What stays on the gateway:

| Left on the gateway | Marked with | After uninstalling |
|---|---|---|
| the `Api` entities | `created_by`, `apim_api_id`, `deployment_ref` | served; remove `created_by` to manage them as any Otoroshi API |
| the `ApiSubscription`s and their keys | `id_ref` (the application), `apim_subscription_id` | served, managed by hand |
| the derived JWT verifiers, CA certificates, auth modules, application certificates | `created_by`, `apim_derived` | referenced by the plans: keep them |
| the keys the gateway minted for the consumers of jwt, mtls, oauth2-remote and keyless plans | `created_by: apikey-extractor` | Otoroshi's own |
| the data exporter `apim_ingester_exporter` | `apim_managed_by` | its ingester is gone: delete it |

The markers left on the entities are inert: Otoroshi only reads `created_by` on an `Api`. To keep
what only the product holds — documentation, snapshots, the catalogue, the applications — export
each workspace before dropping the databases (`apim export <ws>`, see the [CLI](../gitops/cli.mdx),
or **Workspace settings → Export / import**): the export is importable into another installation.
