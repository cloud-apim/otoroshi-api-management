# Who sees what in the console

The rights are enforced by the **admin API**, not by the screens: the console and the CLI are two
clients of the same API, and an action someone is not allowed answers `403` whatever the client.
A personal API token acts as its owner, with exactly the same rights.

## Who is who

| Level | Who | Where it applies |
|---|---|---|
| **Installation administrator** | the bootstrap administrators (`APIM_BOOTSTRAP_ADMINS`), and the users created as *admin* in Global settings | everywhere: the installation, and every workspace as its administrator |
| **Workspace administrator** | a member of the workspace with the `admin` role | that workspace |
| **Producer** | a member with the `producer` role | that workspace; manages what its teams own |
| **Consumer** | a member with the `consumer` role | the developer portal of the workspace only |

Inside a workspace, **teams** own APIs and applications. A producer manages the APIs of the teams
it belongs to (and the APIs that belong to no team), and sees the credentials of the applications
of its teams. A team has **owners** — the person who creates it is one — and members.

## What is worth knowing

- **Every API is visible to every producer of the workspace**: a producer opens the APIs of the
  other teams read only — the console says so on the API and hides the actions.
- **Declarative documents follow the same rule** (`POST …/apply`, `apim apply`): a producer creates
  APIs for its own teams, and changes, publishes or deploys an existing API only if it manages it.
  Remote catalogs are a workspace administrator's.
- **Deciding on a subscription** belongs to who manages its API; **seeing its secret** belongs to
  the team of its application. Anyone else sees the secret masked. Every read of credentials, and
  every read of the request log, is written in the audit trail.
- **Teams**: any producer creates one, and becomes its owner. Only its owners and the workspace
  administrators rename it and choose its members — otherwise anyone could join a team and manage
  what it owns. Deleting a team is a workspace administrator's.
- **Users** are global. A workspace administrator only sees the users of the workspaces it
  administers; an installation administrator sees them all.
- **The gateway pages** are the installation's: everyone signed in sees the gateway status and the
  projection queue of the whole installation, and the drift reports of their own workspaces. The
  connection settings, the data exporter and the retry of a task are an installation
  administrator's.
- **The audit trail** of a workspace is open to its producers and administrators. The audit of the
  whole installation is read through the API (`GET /api/audit`), by an installation administrator.
- **Consumers** work on the [developer portal](../developer-portal.mdx). What they see there — which
  APIs and plans — is the visibility and the access tags of each API and plan (`user:…`,
  `team:…`, `role:…`), not the roles above.

:::note
The console does not hide every tab a producer cannot use yet: in the workspace settings, Remote
catalogs, Export / import and the editing of Metadata, General and Developer portal are refused by
the API (`403`) rather than hidden.
:::
