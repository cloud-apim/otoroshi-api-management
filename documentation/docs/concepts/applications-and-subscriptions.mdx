# Applications and subscriptions

## Applications

An application groups the subscriptions and the technical identity of a consumer: name,
owning team, the **expected `client_id`** for `jwt`, `mtls` and `oauth2` plans, client certificates
and [metadata](./metadata.mdx).

It groups **identity, never counters**: each credential keeps its own quotas.

### Client certificates

An application lists the certificates it presents on `mtls` plans — the certificate only, never
its private key; the console and the portal read each one (subject, issuer, validity) and offer
its `CN` as the expected `client_id`, which is what the plans that read the CN identify the
application as.

While the application has an **active subscription to an mtls plan**, its certificates are
derived as certificates of the gateway — marked as the product's, removed when no such
subscription is active any more — so that the TLS handshake accepts them, a self-signed one in
particular. Suspending or closing the subscription therefore removes the trust in a self-signed
certificate; a certificate signed by a CA the gateway trusts (the trusted CAs of the plan) is not
affected.

On these plans the subscription carries the `client_id` the application declares: the gateway
serves that client id, and no other, while the subscription is active. Two applications cannot
subscribe to the same plan with the same `client_id`; changing it moves the subscriptions of the
application to the new one.

**Whoever can see an application can see its credentials** — the members of its team, and the
workspace administrators. Reading credentials is recorded in the audit trail.

## Subscriptions

A subscription binds an application to a plan. Its states:

```
                 ┌── auto ──────────────────────────────▶ active ◀──▶ suspended
request ─────────┤                                          │             │
                 ├── manual ─▶ pending ─▶ accepted ─────────┘             │
                 │                     └▶ rejected                        │
                 └── paid ───▶ pending_payment ─▶ payment confirmed ──▶ active
                                       active, suspended ─ end date ─▶ expired ─ renewed ─▶ active
                                                    any open state ──▶ closed
```

A producer can give a subscription an **end date**. From that day on it **expires**: it stays on the
gateway, its keys disabled — the consumer's calls are refused. A later end date, or none, renews it
with the same keys; closing it removes it. The workers expire what is due every minute. A paid
subscription ends with its subscription at the payment provider: it takes no end date.

The approval workflow belongs to the product: Otoroshi carries the plan's validation as a
label only. At request time the product checks that the plan is published and subscribable, visible
to the requester, that the usage statement and the terms are given when asked, that a `client_id`
is declared for token-based plans, and that the application has no open subscription to that plan.

## Credentials

A subscription becomes an Otoroshi apikey — **only once it is effective**: a subscription waiting
for a producer or a payment is not on the gateway at all.

- On `apikey` and `oauth2-local` plans the gateway mints a client id and secret; the product reads
  them back and shows them in the portal and in the console.
- On the other kinds the consumer calls with its own token or certificate; nothing is minted
  (the client certificates of the application are derived while the subscription is active, see
  above).
- **Rotate secret** replaces the secret on the gateway at once.
- **Suspend** disables the key; **resume** enables it again.
- **Close** deletes the subscription from the gateway, and Otoroshi revokes its key with it.

Every operation is projected asynchronously and replayed if the gateway is unreachable: the
console shows the projection state (`synced`, `waiting`, `error`) and offers to replay it.

## Transfer

A subscription can be **transferred to an equivalent plan** — same auth mechanism — on the
same API or on another version of it. The key does not change, the subscription keeps its identity
and its history, and Otoroshi applies the target plan's limits to the existing key.

That single operation covers a tier upgrade, the replacement of a badly designed plan, a
renegotiated contract, demoting an abusive consumer instead of cutting it off, and moving to a new
version. Transferring between different mechanisms (apikey → jwt) is refused: the credential would
change, so it is a new subscription.

### Consent

A transfer that degrades the conditions is a change of contract, so it needs the consumer's
agreement. The product compares the two plans and treats as a degradation: a lower rate
limit, a lower daily or monthly quota, a higher price, different terms of use, or access limited
to fewer resources.

| Who starts it | Nothing degrades | Something degrades |
|---|---|---|
| a producer (console, CLI) | applied at once | waits for the consumer's consent, with the reasons |
| a consumer (portal, *Change plan*) | the producer applies or declines it | the request itself carries the consent |

The consent — who, when, for which reasons — is kept in the transfer history of the subscription.
Both sides are notified in-app (the bells of the console and of the portal).

```bash
bun cli/apim.js subscriptions transfer default sub_01a1… --plan plan_partner
```

The command answers with the subscription: when `pending_transfer.status` is `awaiting_consent`,
the move waits for the consumer.

## Notifications

Consumers are notified of what happens to their subscriptions — accepted, rejected, suspended,
resumed, closed, moved, a move to agree to — and producers of what they have to decide — a
request waiting for review, a move asked by a consumer. Notifications are addressed to teams:
every member sees them, and **each reads them and marks them done on their own** — one member
dealing with a request does not hide it from the others.

In the console, the menu of the person signed in shows the unread ones (a dot on the avatar, their
count); opening the menu reads nothing. A notification is read when it is opened or marked read,
and **done** when dealt with: it leaves the list. **All notifications** lists them, to deal with,
unread, done, or all. The admin API serves the same: `GET …/notifications?filter=open|unread|done|all`,
`POST …/notifications/read` (`{"ids": […]}`, all of them without), `POST …/notifications/done`
(`{"ids": […], "done": true}`).

## Reviewing

The console lists subscriptions per API, per application and across the workspace, with the
request (usage statement, terms acceptance, request form answers). A reviewer accepts or rejects
with a message; the decision is recorded on the subscription and in the audit trail.

```bash
bun cli/apim.js subscriptions list default --status pending
bun cli/apim.js subscriptions accept default sub_01a1… --message "welcome"
```
